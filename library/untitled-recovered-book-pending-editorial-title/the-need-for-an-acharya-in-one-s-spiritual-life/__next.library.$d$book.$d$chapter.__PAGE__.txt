5:T4dc,The need for an Āchārya in one's spiritual life

One who desires moksham must seek out an Āchārya. When one seeks out an Āchārya and surrenders at their feet, any sin will not pose any hindrance to moksham. Without an Āchārya, regardless of how many good deeds we may commit in our lifetime, we will not be eligible for moksham.
Here are a few anecdotes that elucidate the role of an Āchārya in our lives.

Vishṇu Dharmam tells the story of Kshethrabandhu, who retorted to evil ways but was reformed by a sage. The sage was pleased by Kshethrabandhu's attention span and advised him to chant the name of Govinda, and that one day, the Lord would show him the path of righteousness, reforming him. Kshethrabandhu followed the sage's advice and gained Paramapadam.

Padmottara Purāṇam tells the story of Pundarika, who relentlessly practiced bhakti-yoga but could not get a glimpse of the Lord and hence became dejected. One day, Pundarika encountered Devarishi Nārada and explained to him what plight he was facing. Devarishi Nārada told Pundarika not to worry and gave him the Ashtaakshara Mantram, instructing him to chant and meditate upon it. There are many reasons why the Lord is attributed to our first Āchārya. The following can be considered the most significant reasons:
a) The Lord taught the Vedas to Lord Brahmā.
b) The Lord revealed the Dwaya Mantram to a scared Mahālakshmī when Madhu-Kaithaba stole the Vedas.
c) Through the teachings propagated to Lord Brahmā, He made sure that man reaps the benefit of the Vedas.
d) The Lord graced the four sons of Lord Brahmā- Sanatkumāra, Sanaka, Sananda, and Sanatsujata, and through them, He propagated Hita.
e) He is present as the Antharyami (the one who governs from inside) of great souls like Devarishi Nārada, Parāshara, and Parankusha.
f) The Lord incarnated as Hamsa, Hayagriva, Nara-Nārāyaṇa, Sri Krishna, and propagated Tattva-Hita-Purushārtha.
g) He made a great soul, such as Bhīshma, give the world great teachings such as the Neeti Shāstra.
h) At the end of Dwapara Yugam, when the teachings of the Pancharatra Agamas were lost, the Lord reincarnated as Sankarshana to re-establish them.
i) To establish the Āchārya-Sishya relationship, He incarnated as Nara-Nārāyaṇa and taught Nara the Tirumantram to Nara at Badrinath.
j) As access to the Vedas became restricted, the Lord, through the Āzhwārs, gave the world the Nalayira Divya Prabandhaṅgaḷ or the Tamil Veda, which propagated the message of the Vedas in a local language.

The Āchārya must first master the three divine secrets and their meaning in their entirety. Only after learning it in its entirety is he supposed to pass it on to his pupils. The Āchārya must perform Pañcha-Saṃskāram, which includes initiation to the three divine mantras, namely the Tirumantram, Dwaya Mantram, and Charama Shloka.
The Āchārya must impart in privacy to the disciple seeking moksham the inner meanings of the three Rahasyams. The disciple, in turn, must reflect with utmost concentration the implications of the mantras and how they can conduct themselves accordingly to attain moksham. Living per the Āchārya's instruction will relieve one from all sin and grant them a state free from fear about anything.
We must understand that the Lord and Mahālakshmī are the controllers of creation, sustenance, and destruction of all Chith and Achith. Knowing the Lord's unique position, we must try only to seek Him out and nobody else.
